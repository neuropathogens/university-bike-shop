In brief
Capital is allocated to where each renovation dollar earns the most return, not spread evenly across a wish list.
  • 01Every scope item is scored on yield-on-cost and stabilized value created; the optimizer selects the marginal dollar against the budget and stops where it clears the hurdle.
  • 02The efficient frontier, leverage, rent ceiling, synergy bundles, category guardrails, phasing, and spend elasticity all compose in one solve — with a downside-robust scope behind it.
  • 03The single-asset solve becomes the subroutine inside a fund-level allocation that spreads portfolio capital across deals to maximize total value created.

YoC = incremental NOI ÷ cost. In-unit premiums are capped at the comp-supported market rent ceiling, with headroom allocated to the most rent-efficient upgrades first — so stacking kitchen + bath + flooring + W/D never claims more premium than the submarket pays, and the marginal upgrade past the ceiling earns no incremental rent. Value created = ΔNOI ÷ exit cap − cost (instant equity at stabilization; negative = destroys value, flagged red). Item IRR and NPV @ hurdle reflect renovation downtime (in-place rent forgone while units are offline, set per item via Down mo) and turnover-based realization (in-unit premiums ramp in as leases roll over the annual turnover window, not all at once) — so a lift starting month 3 beats the same lift in month 18, and a scope that takes units offline is charged the rent it costs to execute. Break-even back-solves the controllable lever (rent / income / cost ceiling) at which a flagged item clears. The optimizer maximizes an additive objective (value created, NPV @ hurdle, or levered NPV); per-item IRR drives ranking and the marginal stop, since IRRs don't sum across a portfolio. Levered equity IRR funds the reno capex at the chosen LTC and rate (interest-only carry on the drawn balance, principal repaid at exit); downtime stays equity-funded. When the asset yield exceeds the debt cost, leverage is positive and lifts equity returns — switch the objective to Levered NPV to let cheap debt pull sub-hurdle items above the line. The capex budget is the binding constraint either way, and value created stays leverage-independent. Exclusive options and precedence are solved exactly; the frontier sweep relaxes precedence coupling.
Capital Deploymentphased tranches · peak equity
Items carry a Phase (tranche); phase k defers its capex and premium ramp by (k−1) × phase gap months. The chart tracks the cumulative equity position — how deep the investor is in at each month — for the recommended scope, against the all-day-one (single-tranche) alternative. The low point is peak equity, the real capital at risk; phasing lifts that floor because NOI from earlier tranches cushions later outlays, so operations self-fund part of the renovation. The tradeoff: deferring a tranche also delays its premium, so phasing trades value-capture speed for capital efficiency — the IRR comparison shows which side wins. Value created is timing-independent, so it is unchanged.
Spend Elasticitydepth per item · diminishing returns
An elastic item isn’t binary — its premium scales with spend along a concave curve (exponent α < 1: spend x× the baseline cost and capture xα× the premium). The optimizer expands each elastic item into a spend-level menu and picks the depth jointly with the rest of the scope and the budget, so it answers not just which upgrades but how far to take each. The value-max marker is the unconstrained best depth; the picked depth can fall short of it when budget pressure makes the marginal dollar worth more on another line — or land below the standard spec when the spec over-improves. Scoped to standalone value/income drivers.
Monte Carlo Riskdistributional · fixed scope
Draws exit cap, in-unit rent confidence, and value confidence from the sensitivity ranges above, plus a right-skewed cost-overrun (overruns more likely than savings), then re-prices the recommended scope across every trial. Reports the distribution of value created and portfolio IRR (P10 / P50 / P90), the probability the scope creates value at all, and each item's probability of staying accretive under uncertainty — the robust scope is the set that survives the distribution, not just the base case. Selection is held at the base-case recommendation; the simulation stress-tests that scope rather than re-optimizing each trial. Per-item P(accretive) is shown for all non-excluded items, so reliably-accretive items the optimizer skipped and coin-flip items it selected both surface.
Hold-Period Sweepre-optimized · per horizon
Re-solves the optimal scope at each hold length from 1 to 7 years, holding all else fixed, and traces how levered & unlevered IRR, equity multiple, and value created move with the exit horizon — then marks the IRR-maximizing hold, the flip horizon where the turnover ramp has fully realized the in-unit premium but the value pop hasn’t yet diluted over time. Under the value objective the optimal scope is hold-invariant (value created is stabilized); switch the optimizer to an NPV objective to watch the scope itself shift — short holds favor quick value realization, long holds favor cash yield. Each horizon is a full re-optimization (groups, precedence, bundles, category constraints, and the rent ceiling all re-applied), not a re-pricing of one fixed scope — so the recommended renovation, not just its return, can change with the hold.

Fund-Level Allocationportfolio · capital across deals
Each saved scenario is a candidate property. The fund allocates its equity budget across the deals to maximize total value created — a second-level optimizer that draws on each deal’s own efficient frontier (value vs capex, converted to equity at that deal’s leverage), then convolves them under the fund budget exactly as the per-asset engine convolves category spend. A deal can be funded to partial scope if its marginal improvements are out-competed; capital flows to the highest value per dollar across the whole portfolio. This tool’s single-asset solve becomes the subroutine inside the portfolio allocation.
