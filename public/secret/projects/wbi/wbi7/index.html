Mezzanine is more leverage at a higher cost. When the deal’s yield exceeds the blended debt cost (positive leverage), adding mezz lifts the equity IRR and shrinks the equity check; when it doesn’t, mezz drags returns. The trade-off is coverage: all-in DSCR and debt yield fall as the stack thickens — lenders and your own risk tolerance cap how thin that can go.

Value-Creation Bridge — Where the Profit Comes From
Splits the deal’s levered profit into the sources that actually produced it — operating income collected over the hold, value from growing NOI, value from cap-rate movement (the exit cap vs your going-in yield), selling costs, and financing (interest paid). It answers the question every committee asks: is this an income deal or a cap-rate bet? The five sources reconcile exactly to the profit shown above.

Mark how sure you are of each driver. 9c · Two-Way Sensitivity Table
The tornado moves one driver at a time; this moves two at once and shows the whole grid of outcomes. Pick any two drivers and a metric — every cell is a complete re-underwrite of the deal at that pair of assumptions, so you can see the interactions a one-way view hides (for example, how much rent growth it takes to offset a softer exit cap). This is the classic data table ARGUS makes you export to Excel to build.
Pick two drivers and a metric, then click Build table.
Each axis steps the chosen driver around its base value in fixed increments; every cell re-runs the full monthly engine. A pure overlay — nothing in your base model changes. Step sizes are fixed (not calibrated to this market) and the grid is discrete.
9d · Break-Even & Margin of Safety
Goal-seek solves for a target return; this solves for the failure thresholds and shows how much room sits between the base case and each one. For every key driver it finds the break-even — where year-1 DSCR hits 1.0× (covenant) or where equity profit hits zero — and reports the cushion. It answers “how far can this fall before it breaks?” Each row is solved by bisection on a full re-underwrite; a pure overlay that changes nothing in your model.
Click to find each break-even and its cushion.
10 · Goal Seek — Solve for a Target
Set a target and click Solve to back into the required input.
Bisection solver: finds the single input value (price, exit cap, or a uniform rent factor) that produces your target return, holding all other assumptions fixed. In ARGUS this requires an Excel export — here it is one click.
10b · Capital-Structure Optimizer
Given your debt terms and lender constraints, this searches senior + mezzanine leverage to find the mix that maximizes the levered equity return — then shows it against your current structure and an all-equity baseline, and names the constraint that pins the optimum. (These constraints are your term sheet: change them and re-run to compare lenders side by side.)
Set your constraints and click Optimize. The senior rate used is the one in the Debt card; the search varies how much senior and mezzanine debt to use.
Grid search over senior and mezzanine leverage (as a % of price), re-running the full engine for each candidate; the senior rate comes from the Debt card, mezzanine at the rate above. Leverage lifts the return while the deal’s unlevered return beats the cost of debt, until a constraint binds; if debt costs more than the deal yields, the optimum carries little or no leverage. Directional (a discrete grid), and the optimizer doesn’t re-price debt by leverage tier — real lenders do.
10c · Pre-Mortem — Kill-Zone Analysis
The opposite of the tornado: instead of the spread of outcomes, this asks what makes the deal lose money. It finds each driver’s kill point (how far it must move adversely, on its own, to drop the return to your bar — or “robust” if it can’t sink the deal alone) and the combinations of drivers that fail even when neither move alone would. Knowing your specific failure modes is more actionable than knowing your variance.
Set your failure bar (0% = literally lose money; or use your hurdle rate) and click Run.
Each kill point is found by bisection (the driver is monotone in its adverse direction); combinations apply a moderate adverse “notch” to two drivers at once. A pure overlay — nothing in your base model changes. The notch sizes are fixed moderate stresses, not calibrated to this market.
10d · Version Diff — Git for Deals
Snapshot the deal at any point, then compare two versions to see exactly which assumptions changed and how each change moved the levered IRR. The attribution replaces one group of assumptions at a time and re-runs the full model, so each figure is that group’s standalone effect; because assumptions interact, the parts don’t sum exactly to the total — the interaction residual is shown explicitly.

10e · Actuals vs Underwriting
Once the deal is operating, enter the actual results year by year and compare them to what you underwrote. The tool reports per-year NOI variance ($ and %), a cumulative NOI index (actual ÷ underwritten), and an ahead / on-track / behind flag against your threshold. The underwriting it compares against is the model’s current projection — so snapshot the original underwriting first if you’ll keep editing assumptions.

Welcome to Wang Brothers Investments — In-House ARGUS

An institutional commercial-real-estate DCF underwriting tool — lease-level, monthly cash flows, levered & unlevered returns — running entirely in your browser. No server, no account; nothing leaves your machine.

How it works
  1. Start from a scenario — pick one of 25 California presets (top-left), or choose Custom for a blank deal.
  2. Edit assumptions — rent roll, leasing, expenses, debt, exit. The KPI strip and charts update live as you type.
  3. Analyze & export — run sensitivity, the optimizer, or the pre-mortem; export an IC summary, a slideshow deck, or Excel.
