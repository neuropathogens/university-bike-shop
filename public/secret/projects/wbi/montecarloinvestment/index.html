XI. Index of Statistical Terms

TermMeaning
Monte Carlo simulationRunning the same financial model thousands of times, each time with randomly drawn values for the uncertain inputs, to see the full range of possible outcomes rather than a single guess.
TrialOne complete run of the model with one random draw of every uncertain input.
Probability distributionA description of which values an uncertain input can take and how likely each is. Defined by its shape and parameters rather than a single number.
Normal distributionThe symmetric bell curve, set by a mean (center) and standard deviation (spread). Used here for rent growth, expense growth, interest rate, and the starting-rent multiplier.
Triangular distributionDefined by a minimum, most-likely, and maximum value. Useful when you can only estimate a best, likely, and worst case. Used for exit cap, turnover, stabilization time, uplift, legal, and buyouts.
Beta distributionA distribution bounded between 0 and 1 (i.e., 0–100%), so it can be skewed without producing impossible values. Used for vacancy.
Lognormal distributionA distribution whose logarithm is normal; it cannot go below zero and has a long right tail. Used for repair overruns and insurance shocks, where small overshoots are common and huge ones are rare but possible.
Correlation (Gaussian copula)A way of making two random inputs move together. Here, rent growth and exit cap can be linked (default −0.3: strong rent markets tend to trade at lower caps), so the simulation doesn't naively treat them as independent.
IRR (internal rate of return)The annualized rate of return that sets the present value of all cash flows — money in, money out, and the sale — to exactly zero. The single number summarizing a deal's return over time.
Expected value (mean)The probability-weighted average outcome across all trials. What you would earn on average if you could do this deal many times.
Median (P50)The middle outcome: half of trials did better, half did worse. Less distorted by extreme outliers than the mean.
Standard deviationHow spread out the outcomes are around the mean. Bigger means riskier: outcomes vary more from trial to trial.
Percentile (P5, P25, P75, P95)The value below which that share of trials fall. P5 is a realistic downside (only 5% of outcomes were worse); P95 is a realistic upside.
CVaR / expected shortfallThe average IRR across only the worst 5% of trials. Unlike P5 (the edge of the tail), CVaR tells you how bad it gets once you're inside the tail.
Cumulative distribution (CDF)For any IRR value, the share of trials at or below it. The S-shaped curve in Fig. 2; steep sections are where outcomes cluster.
Equity multipleTotal cash returned (all cash flows plus sale proceeds) divided by cash invested. 2.0× means you doubled your money, ignoring timing.
Probability of losing moneyThe share of trials in which total profit, including the sale, was negative.
Going-in cap rateYear-one NOI divided by total cost. If this is far above the exit cap you assume, the model is quietly claiming you bought below market value — the single most common way simulations overstate returns.
Hurdle rateYour minimum acceptable return. P(IRR < hurdle) is the chance the deal fails to clear that bar.
Risk-adjusted ratio(Mean IRR − hurdle) ÷ standard deviation, a Sharpe-style measure: how much excess return the deal earns per unit of risk taken.
Rank correlation (tornado chart)A measure from −1 to +1 of how strongly an input moves with IRR across the trials. The biggest bars are the inputs that drive most of the risk — get those assumptions right first.
