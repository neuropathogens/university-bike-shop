=== 7|Advanced: Low-Frequency Words I|Low-frequency list, A–F; harder words in the same item formats.

@ C
The new governor ______ to the legislature's demands only after weeks of protest, finally agreeing to fund the schools at last year's level.
* acceded
- abdicated
- abjured
- arrogated
> Finally agreeing to demands means giving in to them: "acceded." "Abdicated" means renouncing a throne or duty.

@ C
Linguists note that the English "night" and the German "Nacht" are ______: both descend from the same ancient root.
* cognate
- coterminous
- concave
- copious
> Words that share an origin are "cognate." "Coterminous" means sharing boundaries.

@ C
The essay's argument is ______; it rambles from childhood memories to tax policy to the history of jazz without ever connecting them.
* discursive
- axiomatic
- categorical
- concordant
> Rambling between unconnected topics is "discursive."

@ C
Scholars long believed that the manuscript was lost, but a copy is ______ in a private library in Lisbon.
* extant
- defunct
- expendable
- errant
> A surviving copy still exists: "extant." "Defunct" means the opposite.

@ C
The team's early success bred ______: after winning ten straight games, the players stopped attending film sessions and lost their next four.
* complacency
- diffidence
- compunction
- consternation
> Becoming self-satisfied and careless after success is complacency (a mid-list word that the low list builds on).

@ C
The chef's ______ hands could debone a fish in under a minute without wasting any flesh.
* dexterous
- callow
- feckless
- flaccid
> Skillful, quick hands are "dexterous."

@ C
The general's memoir ______ his own role in the victory while barely mentioning the officers who planned it.
* aggrandizes
- abases
- adumbrates
- effaces
> Inflating his own role while ignoring others' is "aggrandizing" it. "Effaces" would mean erasing his role.

@ C
The new evidence was ______ to the prosecution's case, directly contradicting the timeline it had presented.
* detrimental
- conducive
- ancillary
- apropos
> Evidence that contradicts the prosecution's timeline harms its case: "detrimental." "Conducive" means helpful.

@ C
Critics called the minister ______ in his response to the flood, noting that he delayed sending aid for three days while he consulted advisers.
* dilatory
- brisk
- ardent
- candid
> Delaying for three days is the meaning of "dilatory" (slow, tending to delay). "Brisk" is the opposite.

@ C
The painter's palette is deliberately ______: every canvas in the series uses only shades of gray.
* monochromatic
- effulgent
- florid
- garish
> Using only shades of gray is the definition of "monochromatic."

@ M
The scandal cast a pall over the celebration, and many guests left early.
? As used in the text, what does the word "pall" most nearly mean?
* gloomy atmosphere
- cloth covering
- loud noise
- formal speech
> Something cast over a celebration that makes guests leave is a mood of gloom. A burial cloth is the literal sense.

@ M
The senator was careful to couch her criticism in polite language so as not to embitter her colleagues.
? As used in the text, what does the word "embitter" most nearly mean?
* make resentful
- poison
- confuse
- silence
> Harsh criticism risks making colleagues resentful; "embitter" means to make bitter in feeling.

@ M
The forest fire burned for weeks, and only the hardiest saplings could fend off the drought that followed.
? As used in the text, what does the phrase "fend off" most nearly mean?
* resist
- provide for
- invite
- predict
> Surviving the drought means withstanding or resisting it. "Fend for" (provide for) is a different idiom.

@ M
The scientist's latest paper has fanned the debate about whether the fossil is a new species.
? As used in the text, what does the word "fanned" most nearly mean?
* intensified
- cooled
- ended
- admired
> To fan a debate, like a fire, is to stir it up or intensify it.

@ T
The columnist's attack on the proposal was ______, filled with insults such as "idiotic" and "a scheme only a fool could love."
* acerbic
- effusive
- equable
- deferential
> Harsh, biting language is "acerbic."

@ T
In contrast to the ______ praise heaped on the film by fans, professional critics responded with polite but limited approval.
* fulsome
- tepid
- captious
- churlish
> "In contrast to" polite but limited approval, the fans' praise must have been excessive: "fulsome." "Tepid" would match the critics.

@ T
The old letters reveal a ______ side of the famously stern general; he signs them "your loving Papa" and fills the margins with drawings of horses.
* doting
- choleric
- bellicose
- captious
> Signing letters "loving Papa" and drawing pictures for his children show excessive fondness: "doting."

@ X
For centuries, scholars assumed that the Voynich manuscript was a hoax. ______, recent analysis shows that its text follows the statistical patterns of natural language.
* Yet
- Thus
- Similarly
- Indeed
> The recent analysis challenges the hoax assumption, so a contrast ("Yet") fits.

@ X
Desert tortoises can survive a year without drinking. ______, they store water in their bladders and reabsorb it as needed.
* To do so,
- Even so,
- Instead,
- Likewise,
> The second sentence explains the method behind the first, so "To do so" fits.

@ X
The study found that students who slept eight hours scored higher than those who slept six. ______, the researchers caution that the students who slept more may also have studied more.
* That said,
- Therefore,
- For example,
- In summary,
> The caution limits the finding, so a mild concession ("That said") fits.

=== 8|Advanced: Low-Frequency Words II|Low-frequency list, G–P; heavy on connotation and secondary meanings.

@ C
The poet's ______ phrasing, with two or three words where others would use ten, has made her lines easy to quote.
* pithy
- garrulous
- prolix
- grandiloquent
> Concise and meaningful phrasing is "pithy." The other three all mean wordy.

@ C
The researchers found the cause of the disease only through ______ work: they tested every water source in the district, one by one, over two years.
* painstaking
- haphazard
- histrionic
- glib
> Testing every source one by one over two years is extremely careful: "painstaking."

@ C
The company's ______ claims about its product, including that it "eliminates all toxins," led regulators to demand evidence.
* grandiose
- modest
- laconic
- impartial
> Sweeping claims such as "eliminates all toxins" are exaggeratedly grand: "grandiose."

@ C
Because the tribe was ______, moving with the seasons to follow herds, it left few permanent structures for archaeologists to study.
* nomadic
- indigenous
- insular
- hereditary
> Moving with the seasons is "nomadic." That explains the lack of permanent structures.

@ C
The court found the company's response to the safety complaints ______, noting that it had received forty warnings before taking any action.
* negligent
- judicious
- meticulous
- immaculate
> Ignoring forty warnings is careless failure of duty: "negligent."

@ C
The detective's ______ questions, which went straight to the inconsistencies in the suspect's story, unsettled him almost immediately.
* incisive
- obtuse
- languid
- inane
> Sharp questions that go straight to the weak points are "incisive."

@ C
Although his opponents ______ him as reckless, the senator's voting record shows that he was, if anything, overly cautious.
* maligned
- lionized
- mollified
- exonerated
> "Although" sets up a contrast between an unfair attack and the facts. "Maligned" means unfairly criticized.

@ C
The town's economy is ______ on a single factory; when it closed, nearly every local business suffered.
* predicated
- impinged
- interposed
- importuned
> An economy based on a single factory is "predicated on" it. None of the other verbs takes "on" with this meaning.

@ C
Her ______ curiosity led her to take apart every clock, radio, and toaster in the house before she was ten.
* insatiable
- palatable
- immaterial
- illusory
> Curiosity that drives her to take apart everything cannot be satisfied: "insatiable."

@ C
The defense attorney argued that the evidence was merely ______: no witness had seen the defendant at the scene, and no physical evidence linked him to it.
* circumstantial
- irrefutable
- incontrovertible
- palpable
> Evidence without eyewitnesses or a physical link is indirect: "circumstantial." The other three would strengthen the case.

@ M
The general's victory was ignominious, won by breaking a truce he had sworn to honor.
? As used in the text, what does the word "ignominious" most nearly mean?
* shameful
- surprising
- narrow
- celebrated
> A victory won by breaking a sworn truce brings disgrace. "Ignominious" means shameful.

@ M
The candidate's opponents tried to paint him as a pawn of wealthy donors.
? As used in the text, what does the word "paint" most nearly mean?
* portray
- decorate
- conceal
- flatter
> To paint someone as something is to portray them that way.

@ M
The new regulations are meant to curb the pollution that has plagued the river for decades.
? As used in the text, what does the word "plagued" most nearly mean?
* troubled
- infected
- cleaned
- defined
> Pollution that has plagued a river has troubled it persistently. The disease sense is too literal.

@ M
The documentary is a meditation on loss, lingering on empty rooms and unanswered letters.
? As used in the text, what does the word "meditation" most nearly mean?
* thoughtful reflection
- relaxation exercise
- religious ritual
- formal argument
> A film that lingers quietly on loss is a reflective exploration of it.

@ T
The reviewer's assessment was ______: she called the novel "competent, inoffensive, and instantly forgettable."
* lukewarm
- euphoric
- vitriolic
- reverential
> "Competent" and "inoffensive" but "forgettable" is mild, unenthusiastic approval: "lukewarm." It is not harsh enough to be "vitriolic."

@ T
The speaker's ______ manner, with long pauses, sighing, and a mournful voice, made even good news sound like a tragedy.
* lugubrious
- jocular
- jovial
- mercurial
> A mournful, exaggeratedly gloomy manner is "lugubrious."

@ T
Some readers find the narrator's ______ insistence on his own intelligence amusing; others find it simply exhausting.
* pompous
- humble
- laconic
- plaintive
> Self-important insistence on one's intelligence is "pompous."

@ X
Marie Curie won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1903. ______, she became the first person to win a second Nobel Prize, this time in Chemistry.
* Eight years later,
- Before that,
- In contrast,
- Instead,
> The second prize (1911) came after the first, so a later-time transition fits.

@ X
Most mammals cannot digest lactose after infancy. ______, many humans, particularly those with ancestors who herded cattle, can digest milk throughout life.
* Notably, however,
- As a result,
- Similarly,
- In other words,
> The humans described are an exception to the general mammalian rule.

@ X
The museum's new wing doubled its gallery space. ______, attendance has increased by only 10 percent.
* Despite this expansion,
- Because of this expansion,
- In a similar way,
- To illustrate,
> Attendance rising only slightly despite doubled space is a concession.

=== 9|Advanced: Low-Frequency Words III|Low-frequency list, Q–Z, plus phrases and idioms that the SAT treats as single units.

@ C
The scientist's findings ______ earlier theories about how birds navigate, suggesting that magnetism plays a role alongside the stars.
* complement
- compliment
- contravene
- requite
> Findings that add a factor "alongside" the stars round out the earlier theories. "Complement" means to complete. "Compliment" means to praise.

@ C
The newly discovered letters ______ the theory that the poet wrote her final collection in hiding.
* attest to
- detract from
- abstain from
- defer to
> Letters that support a theory provide evidence for it: "attest to." The other phrases do not mean "support."

@ C
The witness's statements were ______ the physical evidence: she claimed the car was red, but paint samples showed it was blue.
* at odds with
- in keeping with
- on par with
- in league with
> Red versus blue is a contradiction: "at odds with" means in conflict with.

@ C
The tiny startup managed to ______ much larger rivals, releasing its product six months before theirs.
* outstrip
- waylay
- traduce
- underrate
> Beating larger rivals to market means surpassing them: "outstrip."

@ C
The judge ruled that the old contract was ______ because both parties had signed a new agreement replacing it.
* void
- valid
- tenable
- unassailable
> A contract replaced by a new agreement no longer has legal force: "void."

@ C
The novelist is known for ______ dialogue that captures exactly how teenagers talk, slang and hesitations included.
* naturalistic
- stilted
- turgid
- unctuous
> Dialogue that captures exactly how people talk is realistic: "naturalistic." "Stilted" (stiff, unnatural) is the opposite.

@ C
The rebels hoped to ______ the government's control over the capital by cutting its supply lines.
* undercut
- underscore
- underwrite
- underrate
> Cutting supply lines weakens control: "undercut." "Underscore" means to emphasize.

@ C
Her ______ commitment to the project never wavered, even when funding ran out and she worked for a year without pay.
* unswerving
- vacillating
- venal
- tepid
> Commitment that never wavered is "unswerving."

@ C
The professor ______ students who used the word "literally" for emphasis, reminding them of its actual meaning at every opportunity.
* upbraided
- regaled
- succored
- wheedled
> Repeatedly correcting and scolding students is "upbraiding" them.

@ C
The once-lively debate had become ______; both sides simply repeated their positions without engaging new evidence.
* stagnant
- volatile
- resurgent
- vehement
> A debate that no longer moves forward is "stagnant."

@ M
Officials hope the new law will check the spread of invasive mussels in the lake.
? As used in the text, what does the word "check" most nearly mean?
* halt
- inspect
- verify
- record
> To check a spread is to stop or restrain it. The more common senses (inspect, verify) do not fit a spread.

@ M
The author's account of the expedition is a sober one, free of the heroic exaggeration found in earlier versions.
? As used in the text, what does the word "sober" most nearly mean?
* restrained
- abstaining from alcohol
- sad
- brief
> An account free of exaggeration is measured and restrained.

@ M
The film's budget was modest, but its ambitions were anything but.
? As used in the text, what does the word "modest" most nearly mean?
* limited
- humble
- shy
- proper
> A budget is modest when it is small. "Humble" and "shy" describe people.

@ M
The board decided to table the proposal until the next meeting, when more members could attend.
? As used in the text, what does the word "table" most nearly mean?
* postpone discussion of
- present for discussion
- approve
- reject
> Waiting "until the next meeting" means putting the proposal aside. In American usage, "table" means to postpone.

@ T
The general's ______ reply to the reporter, "Next question," made clear that he would not discuss the failed mission.
* curt
- voluble
- unctuous
- effusive
> A two-word dismissal is rudely brief: "curt."

@ T
The memoir describes the author's childhood poverty without self-pity; its tone is ______ rather than mournful.
* matter-of-fact
- maudlin
- plaintive
- querulous
> "Without self-pity" and "rather than mournful" require a plain, unemotional tone. The other three are all self-pitying.

@ T
The ambassador's speech was ______, carefully praising both nations while committing neither to any specific action.
* diplomatic
- belligerent
- vituperative
- truculent
> Tactful language that avoids commitment is "diplomatic." The others are all aggressive.

@ X
The drug lowered blood pressure in 80 percent of patients. ______, it caused dizziness in nearly half of them.
* At the same time,
- Accordingly,
- For this reason,
- In other words,
> The side effect is presented alongside the benefit as a complicating fact.

@ X
Coast redwoods are the tallest trees in the world. ______, the giant sequoia, a close relative, is the largest by volume.
* However,
- Therefore,
- For instance,
- Likewise,
> The second sentence turns from the record for height to a different tree's record for volume, so a contrast fits.

@ X
Some critics dismissed the novel as a simple adventure story. ______, it has since been recognized as a sharp critique of colonialism.
* In fact,
- Similarly,
- For this reason,
- In addition,
> Later recognition corrects the dismissive view; "In fact" signals the correction.

=== 10|Mastery: Full-Range Review|Mixed top, mid and low items at full SAT difficulty; take it timed (24 minutes) as your final check.

@ C
The historian argues that the treaty's collapse was not ______ but the predictable result of decades of broken promises.
* fortuitous
- inexorable
- foreseeable
- calculable
> "Not X but predictable" requires the opposite of predictable: "fortuitous" (happening by chance).

@ C
Despite her ______ reputation, the reclusive author was warm and talkative in the rare interviews she granted.
* aloof
- gregarious
- affable
- convivial
> "Despite" sets her reputation against her warmth, so the reputation must be cold and distant: "aloof."

@ C
The documentary ______ the popular image of the pirate as a romantic rebel, showing instead the brutal economics of the trade.
* debunks
- corroborates
- embellishes
- extols
> Showing a contrary reality exposes the popular image as false: "debunks."

@ C
Because the ice core's layers are ______, scientists can count them like tree rings to date each year's snowfall.
* discrete
- discreet
- diffuse
- homogeneous
> Countable layers must be separate and distinct: "discrete." "Discreet" means tactful, and "homogeneous" would mean the layers cannot be told apart.

@ C
The economist's model is elegant but ______: it assumes that consumers always act rationally, an assumption few researchers accept.
* tenuous
- robust
- irrefutable
- empirical
> A model resting on a widely rejected assumption is weakly supported: "tenuous." "Robust" is the opposite.

@ C
The young violinist's ______ talent astonished her teachers; at nine, she was performing concertos with professional orchestras.
* precocious
- latent
- mediocre
- nascent
> Talent that is unusually advanced at a young age is "precocious." "Latent" talent has not yet shown itself.

@ C
The agency's report was ______ by an industry lobbyist, who removed several paragraphs about health risks before publication.
* expurgated
- elucidated
- ratified
- corroborated
> Removing objectionable passages from a text is "expurgating" it.

@ C
Rather than ______ his critics, the mayor invited them to join a committee to redesign the plan.
* rebuff
- placate
- consult
- include
> "Rather than" sets inviting critics in against pushing them away: "rebuff." The other three describe what he did instead.

@ C
The fossil record for this period is frustratingly ______: only a handful of specimens survive, most of them incomplete.
* sparse
- copious
- pristine
- ubiquitous
> A handful of incomplete specimens is thin and scattered: "sparse."

@ C
The professor warned that a single anomalous result could not ______ an entire theory, which rested on thousands of consistent observations.
* invalidate
- substantiate
- engender
- augment
> The warning is that one odd result cannot overturn a well-supported theory: "invalidate."

@ M
The scientist's account of her discovery was refreshingly free of affectation; she credited luck as much as skill.
? As used in the text, what does the word "affectation" most nearly mean?
* pretentious display
- emotional attachment
- illness
- influence
> Crediting luck shows that she avoided showing off: "affectation" means artificial behavior meant to impress.

@ M
The negotiators were prepared to concede minor points in order to secure the central agreement.
? As used in the text, what does the word "concede" most nearly mean?
* yield
- admit defeat in
- argue
- reject
> Giving up minor points to win the main agreement is yielding them strategically.

@ M
The townspeople's grievances, long ignored, finally found an outlet in the new council's public hearings.
? As used in the text, what does the word "outlet" most nearly mean?
* means of expression
- retail store
- electrical socket
- exit
> Grievances that find an outlet get a way to be expressed.

@ M
The critic praised the actor's economy of gesture, noting that a single raised eyebrow conveyed more than most actors' tirades.
? As used in the text, what does the word "economy" most nearly mean?
* restraint
- wealth
- financial system
- low cost
> Conveying much with a single gesture is efficient restraint. "Economy" here means sparing use.

@ T
Some reviewers found the novel's ending ______, objecting that its sudden happy reunion felt unearned after four hundred pages of tragedy.
* saccharine
- austere
- sardonic
- ambiguous
> An unearned happy ending that seems overly sweet is "saccharine."

@ T
The scientist's response to her critics was ______, acknowledging the weaknesses in her data while firmly defending her central conclusion.
* measured
- vitriolic
- obsequious
- flippant
> Acknowledging weaknesses while defending the conclusion is balanced and controlled: "measured."

@ T
The editorial's ______ tone, with phrases such as "brazen theft" and "shameless greed," left little doubt about the writer's view of the merger.
* indignant
- ambivalent
- dispassionate
- whimsical
> Words such as "brazen" and "shameless" express anger at perceived injustice: "indignant."

@ X
Bamboo can grow nearly a meter in a single day. ______, it is one of the fastest-growing plants on Earth.
* Indeed,
- Nonetheless,
- In contrast,
- Previously,
> The second sentence reinforces and generalizes the first, so "Indeed" fits.

@ X
The proposal had strong public support. ______, it failed in the legislature by a single vote.
* Nonetheless,
- Consequently,
- Furthermore,
- Specifically,
> Failure despite support is a contrast.

@ X
The first trial of the drug was halted after three patients reported side effects. ______, the second trial used a lower dose and screened participants more carefully.
* Accordingly,
- However,
- Similarly,
- Regardless,
> The second trial's changes respond to the first trial's problems, so "Accordingly" (in response) fits.
