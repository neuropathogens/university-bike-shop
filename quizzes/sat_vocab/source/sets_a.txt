=== 1|Foundations: Top-Frequency Words I|Lessons 1.1–1.5 vocabulary in SAT-style contexts; straightforward clue-and-contrast items.

@ C
Marine biologist Sylvia Earle spent decades arguing that the deep ocean deserved protection. Her early warnings were dismissed by many officials, but ______ surveys of declining fish populations confirmed her concerns, and several nations eventually expanded their marine reserves.
* subsequent
- hypothetical
- occasional
- superior
> The surveys came after her warnings and confirmed them; "subsequent" (coming later) fits the time sequence. "Hypothetical" surveys could not confirm anything.

@ C
Because the new bridge design relied on a lightweight composite that had never been tested in freezing conditions, the engineers were ______ to approve it for the mountain route until further trials were completed.
* hesitant
- eager
- obligated
- inclined
> The untested material gives a reason for caution and delay ("until further trials"), so the engineers were hesitant. "Eager" and "inclined" reverse the logic.

@ C
Historian Ibram X. Kendi notes that the pamphlet's author never stated his motives directly; instead, readers must ______ them from the examples he chose and the arguments he left out.
* infer
- conceal
- impose
- retain
> Motives that are not stated must be worked out from evidence, which is what "infer" means. "Conceal" is what the author did, not the readers.

@ C
The committee's report was praised for being ______: in just four pages, it summarized three years of research without omitting any essential finding.
* concise
- elaborate
- ambiguous
- redundant
> Four pages for three years of work, with nothing essential missing, describes brevity with completeness: "concise." "Elaborate" and "redundant" suggest the opposite.

@ C
Although the drought lasted only a single season, its effects were anything but ______; farmers in the region were still recovering from crop losses nearly a decade later.
* temporary
- severe
- widespread
- harmful
> "Anything but X" plus effects lasting a decade means the effects were not short-lived. Only "temporary" sets up that contrast.

@ C
Early critics described the composer's work as noise, yet within a generation her techniques had become so ______ that film scores routinely borrowed them.
* conventional
- obscure
- hostile
- spontaneous
> "Yet" signals a reversal from rejected to accepted; techniques that film scores routinely borrow have become standard, or conventional.

@ C
To ______ the risk of contaminating the samples, the lab technicians wore sterile gloves and worked inside a sealed cabinet.
* mitigate
- sustain
- predict
- anticipate
> Gloves and a sealed cabinet reduce risk. "Mitigate" means to make less severe. "Anticipate" only means to expect it.

@ C
The novelist's characters rarely announce their feelings. Their emotions are ______, revealed through small gestures, such as a hand withdrawn from a table or a glance held a moment too long.
* subtle
- stark
- candid
- intricate
> Emotions shown only through small gestures are understated and hard to notice: "subtle." "Candid" (frank) contradicts "rarely announce."

@ C
The city council voted to ______ the outdated zoning law, replacing it with rules that allowed housing to be built near transit stations.
* repeal
- endorse
- extract
- negotiate
> Replacing a law means officially cancelling the old one: "repeal." "Endorse" would keep it.

@ C
Researchers found that the medication was effective only under certain conditions; its benefits were ______ on patients taking it with food.
* dependent
- superior
- indispensable
- predictable
> "Only under certain conditions" means the benefits rely on a condition. The idiom is "dependent on." "Indispensable" would need "to."

@ M
Wildlife officials hope that the new corridor will foster contact between isolated groups of mountain lions, increasing genetic diversity in both populations.
? As used in the text, what does the word "foster" most nearly mean?
* encourage
- adopt
- raise as a guardian
- shelter
> The corridor promotes or encourages contact. The "foster care" senses (adopt, raise) don't fit a corridor's effect on contact.

@ M
The startup's founder admitted that the company had foundered after its second product failed to attract customers.
? As used in the text, what does the word "foundered" most nearly mean?
* failed
- established
- discovered
- financed
> "Founder" as a verb means to sink or collapse. The cause given (a product that attracted no customers) points to failure, not founding.

@ M
The museum's spare galleries, with their white walls and single benches, direct every visitor's attention to the paintings themselves.
? As used in the text, what does the word "spare" most nearly mean?
* minimal
- extra
- unused
- generous
> White walls and single benches describe a plain, lean style: "minimal." The common sense "extra" does not describe a room's design.

@ M
Because only two of the sixty respondents had any formal training, the survey's authors added a qualification to their conclusion, noting that the results might not apply to professionals.
? As used in the text, what does the word "qualification" most nearly mean?
* limiting condition
- credential
- certificate
- ability
> The authors limited how broadly their conclusion applies. That is a qualification in the sense of a reservation, not a credential.

@ T
In his review, the critic praised the band's ______ approach to the old folk songs, noting that they had kept the original melodies while adding bold new rhythms.
* innovative
- derivative
- reckless
- mediocre
> The review praises the approach and says new rhythms were added, so a positive word for originality fits: "innovative." "Derivative" is negative and means unoriginal.

@ T
Some townspeople saw the new mayor's frankness as refreshing; others found her a little too ______, complaining that she shared her opinions of colleagues at public meetings.
* candid
- discreet
- hesitant
- diplomatic
> Opponents saw her frankness as excessive, so the word must be close to "frank": "candid." "Discreet" and "diplomatic" are its opposites.

@ T
The scientist's tone in the letter is not angry but ______: she calmly acknowledges her rival's contributions before explaining, point by point, where his data fall short.
* measured
- hostile
- sarcastic
- indifferent
> "Not angry," "calmly," and "point by point" describe a controlled, balanced tone: "measured." "Indifferent" is wrong because she clearly cares enough to argue.

@ X
The ancient city of Petra was carved directly into sandstone cliffs. ______, many of its buildings have survived earthquakes that destroyed freestanding structures elsewhere in the region.
* As a result,
- However,
- Similarly,
- For example,
> Being carved into solid cliffs explains why the buildings survived, so a cause-effect transition fits.

@ X
Honeybees communicate the location of food through a "waggle dance." The angle of the dance indicates direction. ______, the length of each waggle run signals distance.
* Similarly,
- Nevertheless,
- Therefore,
- In contrast,
> Angle encodes direction; length encodes distance. The second fact works the same way as the first, so "Similarly" fits. There is no contrast or cause.

@ X
Many people assume that deserts are always hot. ______, the Gobi Desert regularly records winter temperatures below −30°C.
* In fact,
- Likewise,
- Consequently,
- In addition,
> The second sentence corrects the assumption with a striking counterexample; "In fact" introduces a correcting truth.

=== 2|Foundations: Top-Frequency Words II|Lessons 1.6–1.10 vocabulary; adds secondary meanings and charge (positive/negative) traps.

@ C
The archaeologists could not ______ the age of the pottery fragments until the radiocarbon results arrived, so they labeled each piece "date unknown."
* ascertain
- disregard
- foresee
- denounce
> They needed to find out the age for certain; until then it was unknown. "Ascertain" means to determine.

@ C
Although the new policy affected only a ______ number of students, about a dozen out of several thousand, it attracted weeks of news coverage.
* negligible
- myriad
- prevalent
- noteworthy
> A dozen out of thousands is tiny, and "although" contrasts that small number with heavy coverage: "negligible."

@ C
Rather than choose between the two competing theories, the physicist sought to ______ them, arguing that both could be true at different scales.
* reconcile
- denounce
- disguise
- foresee
> Showing that both theories hold at different scales makes them consistent with each other, which is what "reconcile" means.

@ C
Translator Edith Grossman argued that a good translation is never a mere ______ of the original; it must recreate the voice and rhythm of the source in a new language.
* imitation
- retraction
- salvage
- tenet
> "Never a mere X ... must recreate" contrasts copying with creative rebuilding; "imitation" is the mere copy.

@ C
The documentary avoids taking sides. It presents the arguments of developers and conservationists with equal care, refusing to appear ______.
* partisan
- optimistic
- ignorant
- benign
> A film that avoids taking sides refuses to look biased toward one camp: "partisan."

@ C
The bacteria proved remarkably ______: they survived boiling water, months without nutrients, and doses of radiation that would kill most organisms.
* tenacious
- placid
- erroneous
- uneven
> Surviving extreme conditions shows that the bacteria hold on persistently: "tenacious."

@ C
The author's argument is difficult to follow because his key terms are ______; the word "freedom," for instance, seems to mean something different in every chapter.
* ambiguous
- lucid
- eloquent
- pragmatic
> Terms whose meanings shift are open to multiple interpretations: "ambiguous." "Lucid" is the opposite.

@ C
Hoping to ______ public support for the new library, organizers held readings, invited local authors, and published letters from students who used the old one.
* bolster
- preclude
- speculate
- indulge
> These activities aim to strengthen support: "bolster." "Preclude" would prevent support.

@ C
Some economists argue that sudden tax cuts can ______ inflation, worsening the very price increases that policymakers are trying to control.
* exacerbate
- eradicate
- substantiate
- condone
> "Worsening" restates the blank, so the answer is "exacerbate" (make worse).

@ C
The biography is ______ in its research: the author consulted more than three hundred letters and checked every date against municipal records.
* meticulous
- mundane
- dubious
- ornate
> Consulting hundreds of letters and checking every date shows extreme care with detail: "meticulous."

@ M
The quarterback's performance in the final game did much to vindicate the coach's controversial decision to start him over the veteran.
? As used in the text, what does the word "vindicate" most nearly mean?
* justify
- punish
- question
- replace
> A strong performance showed that the coach's choice was right. "Vindicate" means to justify or prove correct.

@ M
The senator's calm, reasonable tone in the debate belied the anger she had expressed in private memos.
? As used in the text, what does the word "belied" most nearly mean?
* disguised
- confirmed
- explained
- exaggerated
> Her calm tone gave a false impression that hid her private anger. "Belie" means to misrepresent or contradict, not to confirm.

@ M
Long hours at the telescope sharpened the astronomer's discernment, and she learned to tell a distant galaxy from a smudge on the lens at a glance.
? As used in the text, what does the word "discernment" most nearly mean?
* ability to judge precisely
- sense of humor
- level of patience
- eyesight
> Telling a galaxy from a smudge requires fine judgment. "Discernment" is perceptive judgment, not eyesight alone.

@ M
The committee will entertain proposals from any resident before deciding how to use the vacant lot.
? As used in the text, what does the word "entertain" most nearly mean?
* consider
- amuse
- host
- perform
> A committee that entertains proposals is willing to consider them, which is the secondary sense of the word.

@ T
The memoir's tone is often ______, as the author pokes fun at her own early failures as a chef, including a soufflé that "collapsed with dignity."
* self-deprecating
- arrogant
- morbid
- vindictive
> Poking fun at one's own failures is self-deprecating humor. "Arrogant" is the opposite charge.

@ T
The reviewer called the film's ending ______, objecting that its last-minute rescue and tearful reunion had been designed to manipulate the audience's emotions.
* sentimental
- austere
- understated
- ambiguous
> Objecting to an ending engineered to produce tears points to excessive emotion, a negative sense of "sentimental."

@ T
Far from being ______, the professor's comments in the margins were warm and encouraging. "Wonderful start," she wrote, "now push this idea further."
* disparaging
- supportive
- insightful
- lengthy
> "Far from being X" plus warm, encouraging comments means X is the opposite of supportive: "disparaging."

@ X
Electric vehicles produce no tailpipe emissions. ______, the electricity used to charge them may come from coal-burning power plants.
* However,
- Therefore,
- Likewise,
- As a result,
> The second sentence qualifies the first sentence's environmental advantage, so a contrast is needed.

@ X
The Mayan civilization developed a sophisticated calendar. ______, they tracked the movements of Venus with an error of only about two hours over five centuries.
* For example,
- On the other hand,
- Instead,
- Even so,
> Tracking Venus precisely is a specific example of their calendrical sophistication.

@ X
The study included only 40 participants, all from a single university. ______, its findings should be treated as preliminary.
* Thus,
- Nonetheless,
- Similarly,
- By contrast,
> The small, narrow sample is the reason to treat the findings cautiously, so a conclusion transition fits.

=== 3|Foundations: Top-Frequency Words III|Lessons 1.11–1.15 vocabulary; the harder end of the top list.

@ C
The once-thriving railroad town became nearly ______ after the line closed; its population fell from four thousand to fewer than two hundred.
* defunct
- prolific
- venerable
- resolute
> A town that lost almost all of its people has nearly ceased to function: "defunct."

@ C
Opponents of the plan called the proposed fees ______, arguing that they were so high that most small farmers would be unable to pay them.
* prohibitive
- negligible
- gratuitous
- curative
> Fees so high that people cannot pay them effectively prevent participation: "prohibitive."

@ C
Despite repeated setbacks, the expedition team remained ______, and they set out again the following spring with better equipment and a new route.
* undaunted
- complacent
- sullen
- disgruntled
> Setting out again after setbacks shows that they were not discouraged: "undaunted." "Despite" signals the contrast.

@ C
The chemist warned that the solvent was more ______ than it appeared: harmless in a single exposure, it caused lasting damage when inhaled over many months.
* pernicious
- innocuous
- precipitous
- tenuous
> Harm that works gradually and subtly is "pernicious." "Innocuous" (harmless) is the false appearance being contrasted.

@ C
The poet's late work is famously ______; even scholars disagree about what the shortest lyrics mean, and some lines have no agreed interpretation at all.
* inscrutable
- laconic
- strident
- diminutive
> Meaning that even experts cannot agree on is impossible to interpret: "inscrutable." "Laconic" describes brevity, not difficulty.

@ C
Rather than confront the board directly, the manager took a ______ approach, gathering quiet support from individual members before presenting her proposal.
* circumspect
- flagrant
- impetuous
- flippant
> Avoiding direct confrontation and gathering support quietly is cautious and careful: "circumspect." "Impetuous" (rash) is its opposite.

@ C
The essay ______ two scenes, a crowded harvest festival and an empty field in winter, to show how the same village can feel welcoming and lonely.
* juxtaposes
- impugns
- stymies
- supplants
> Placing two contrasting scenes side by side is "juxtaposing" them.

@ C
The diplomat's reply was ______: asked whether talks would continue, she said only, "We'll see."
* terse
- effusive
- bombastic
- loquacious
> Two words in reply to a major question is brief to the point of curtness: "terse." The other three all mean wordy or gushing.

@ C
Because a gene's effect can be masked by its partner, a ______ trait may skip a generation before reappearing in grandchildren.
* recessive
- defunct
- tangential
- somber
> In genetics, a trait that is masked when paired with a dominant gene is "recessive."

@ C
New evidence about the fire's cause led the investigators to ______ the night watchman, who had been blamed for leaving a lamp burning.
* vindicate
- usurp
- censure
- consecrate
> Evidence about the real cause clears a person who was wrongly blamed: "vindicate." "Censure" would blame him further.

@ M
After decades of debate, the theory began to ossify into dogma, and few young researchers dared to question its assumptions.
? As used in the text, what does the word "ossify" most nearly mean?
* become rigid
- grow stronger
- turn into bone
- become popular
> Ideas that harden into unquestioned dogma have become fixed and inflexible. The literal sense, turning into bone, does not apply to ideas.

@ M
The essay is redolent of the author's childhood in coastal Maine, full of salt air, fog, and the clank of lobster traps.
? As used in the text, what does the word "redolent" most nearly mean?
* strongly suggestive
- critical
- forgetful
- smelling sweet
> The essay evokes or suggests her childhood. The literal sense, fragrant, does not fit an essay.

@ M
The novel's narrator is a cloying presence who repeatedly insists that every character is "simply wonderful."
? As used in the text, what does the word "cloying" most nearly mean?
* sickeningly sweet
- deeply honest
- frightening
- forgettable
> Excessive, insistent sweetness ("simply wonderful") becomes tiresome. "Cloying" means sickeningly sweet through excess.

@ M
The general's coup was an attempt to usurp the authority that the constitution granted only to elected officials.
? As used in the text, what does the word "usurp" most nearly mean?
* seize wrongfully
- share equally
- give up
- strengthen
> A coup takes power that the general is not entitled to. "Usurp" means to seize wrongfully.

@ T
Critics found the speech ______, complaining that its grand phrases, such as "a destiny written in the stars of history," contained little actual policy.
* bombastic
- trenchant
- prosaic
- laconic
> Grand-sounding language with little substance is "bombastic." "Trenchant" would be a compliment.

@ T
The host was famous for her ______ gatherings, at which guests lingered past midnight trading stories and laughing.
* convivial
- somber
- austere
- decorous
> Lingering, story-trading, and laughter describe lively, friendly company: "convivial."

@ T
Once a ______ critic of the museum, the columnist changed her tone entirely after its renovation, calling the new galleries "a gift to the city."
* caustic
- magnanimous
- reticent
- phlegmatic
> "Changed her tone entirely" to praise means that she was formerly harsh. "Caustic" means sharply critical.

@ X
Octopuses can change color in milliseconds. ______, they appear to be colorblind, which has puzzled biologists for decades.
* Paradoxically,
- Accordingly,
- For instance,
- Moreover,
> Color-changing animals that cannot see color is a seeming contradiction; "Paradoxically" signals it.

@ X
The first draft of the treaty was rejected by both sides. ______, a revised version that addressed water rights was signed within a year.
* Eventually, however,
- Therefore,
- In other words,
- Specifically,
> The outcome reverses the rejection after time passes, so a later-time contrast fits.

@ X
Spiders produce several types of silk. Dragline silk is used for frames and safety lines; ______, capture silk, which is stretchy and sticky, is used to trap prey.
* by comparison,
- as a result,
- in conclusion,
- for this reason,
> The sentence sets a second silk type beside the first, with different properties. "By comparison" introduces the parallel contrast.
