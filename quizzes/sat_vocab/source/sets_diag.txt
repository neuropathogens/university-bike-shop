=== 0|Diagnostic|Eight items, one per trap type in Section 5.

@ C
The new trail map is ______; first-time hikers can follow it without a guide, and every junction is clearly labeled.
* intelligible
- intricate
- obscure
- elaborate
> Restatement clue: "can follow it without a guide." "Intricate" and "elaborate" are related-but-imprecise traps, since a detailed map could still be confusing.

@ C
Although the senator's speech was ______, filled with sweeping promises, it contained almost no specific proposals.
* grandiose
- terse
- pragmatic
- tentative
> "Although" sets sweeping promises against a lack of specifics. "Terse" and "tentative" reverse the direction; "pragmatic" has the wrong charge.

@ M
The museum will not countenance any photography in the manuscript room.
? As used in the text, what does the word "countenance" most nearly mean?
* permit
- face
- notice
- display
> Secondary-meaning trap: the familiar sense "face" does not fit. As a verb, "countenance" means to tolerate or permit.

@ C
The fossil is so well preserved that researchers could ______ the animal's last meal from the contents of its stomach.
* deduce
- devour
- disclose
- deride
> Researchers work out the meal from evidence, so "deduce" fits. "Devour" is a sound-alike trap from the "meal" context.

@ T
Reviewers who expected a dry history were surprised by the book's ______ tone; its author jokes about kings and footnotes alike.
* irreverent
- solemn
- reverent
- vindictive
> Joking about kings shows a lack of reverence. "Solemn" and "reverent" have the wrong charge; "vindictive" is negative in the wrong way.

@ C
The committee's decision to ______ the rule was not a rejection of its goals; members simply wanted time to study its costs.
* suspend
- abolish
- denounce
- repudiate
> "Not a rejection," plus "wanted time," points to a temporary halt. The other three are too strong: they are permanent or hostile.

@ X
Most comets orbit the Sun in long ellipses. ______, Halley's Comet returns roughly every 76 years.
* For example,
- However,
- Instead,
- In conclusion,
> Halley's Comet illustrates the general claim about comet orbits.

@ C
The scientist's claim was ______ the data: her own table showed the opposite trend.
* at odds with
- in line with
- borne out by
- consistent with
> In this phrase trap, the opposite trend means a contradiction, so the answer is "at odds with." The other three phrases all mean agreement.
