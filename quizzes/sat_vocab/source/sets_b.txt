=== 4|Building: Mid-Frequency Words I|Lessons 2.1–2.15; the academic workhorse words the SAT uses most in science and social-science texts.

@ C
To ______ the movement of goods between the two ports, the government built a rail line and cut customs fees in half.
* facilitate
- prohibit
- derive
- undergo
> Building a rail line and cutting fees make trade easier: "facilitate."

@ C
The archive's collection is far from ______; the earliest letters were lost in a 1906 fire, and several decades are missing entirely.
* exhaustive
- partial
- preliminary
- arbitrary
> Lost letters and missing decades mean that the collection is incomplete. "Far from" requires the opposite of incomplete, which is "exhaustive." "Partial" would describe the collection as it actually is.

@ C
Several smaller parties formed a ______ in order to win a majority in parliament, since none of them could govern alone.
* coalition
- statute
- hierarchy
- paradigm
> Groups joining forces to win a majority form an alliance: "coalition."

@ C
The researchers' conclusions were ______, based on direct measurements of soil chemistry rather than on computer models or assumptions.
* empirical
- subjective
- hypothetical
- intangible
> Based on direct measurement or observation means "empirical." "Hypothetical" is the opposite.

@ C
Because the river's flow is ______, heavy during spring melt and nearly dry by August, farmers along its banks store water in large tanks.
* intermittent
- perpetual
- homogeneous
- finite
> A flow that is heavy at one time of year and nearly dry at another stops and starts: "intermittent." "Perpetual" contradicts "nearly dry."

@ C
The historian was ______ of the diary's authenticity, noting that its paper was manufactured decades after the events it describes.
* skeptical
- reputable
- exemplary
- prone
> Paper made after the events described gives a reason to doubt the diary: "skeptical of."

@ C
The new microscope's resolution allowed biologists to ______ structures inside the cell that had previously been invisible.
* discern
- disclaim
- surpass
- contradict
> Making formerly invisible structures visible lets biologists perceive or detect them: "discern."

@ C
The artist's style is ______, combining Japanese woodblock techniques, Mexican muralism, and the bold colors of 1960s pop art.
* eclectic
- pristine
- ironic
- perennial
> Drawing on several diverse sources is the definition of "eclectic."

@ C
Smartphones have become so ______ that it is now difficult to find a city street where no one is looking at one.
* ubiquitous
- fugitive
- terrestrial
- fraudulent
> Found everywhere, so that no street lacks one: "ubiquitous."

@ C
The coach's decision to rest her best players proved ______: the team lost the game, and with it the chance to host the playoffs.
* consequential
- humane
- latent
- exemplary
> The decision had major results, a loss and a missed home playoff: "consequential."

@ M
Investors were encouraged by the prospect of lower interest rates later in the year.
? As used in the text, what does the word "prospect" most nearly mean?
* likelihood
- search
- customer
- view
> Investors are encouraged by the possibility of a future event. "Prospect" means likelihood here, not a scenic view or a mining search.

@ M
The board refused to sanction the expansion until an independent study confirmed that it would not harm the wetland.
? As used in the text, what does the word "sanction" most nearly mean?
* approve
- penalize
- delay
- fund
> "Sanction" can mean both "penalize" and "approve." Here the board withholds permission until a study is done, so it means approve.

@ M
The court ruled that the evidence did not warrant a new trial.
? As used in the text, what does the word "warrant" most nearly mean?
* justify
- guarantee
- authorize an arrest
- delay
> Evidence that does not warrant a new trial does not justify one. This is the verb sense of the word.

@ M
Several critics have observed that the novelist's later books appreciate the humor in ordinary life more fully than her early, darker work.
? As used in the text, what does the word "appreciate" most nearly mean?
* recognize
- increase in value
- thank
- enjoy a meal
> The books recognize, or fully perceive, the humor. "Increase in value" is the financial sense and does not fit.

@ T
The senator's ______ remarks about the new law, which called it "a gift to those who need it least," angered its supporters.
* sardonic
- cordial
- tentative
- reverent
> Calling a law "a gift to those who need it least" mocks it bitterly. "Sardonic" captures that biting, ironic tone.

@ T
Unlike her ______ predecessor, who rarely left his office, the new principal visits classrooms daily and eats lunch with students.
* aloof
- gregarious
- prudent
- avid
> The contrast with a principal who visits classrooms and eats with students requires a distant predecessor: "aloof."

@ T
The author's description of the factory is ______: she lists the machines, the shift schedules, and the wages without expressing any opinion about them.
* neutral
- nostalgic
- ironic
- frantic
> Listing facts without opinion is a neutral, objective tone.

@ X
Venus is often called Earth's twin because the two planets are similar in size. ______, conditions on Venus are drastically different: its surface temperature exceeds 460°C.
* Nevertheless,
- Furthermore,
- Hence,
- Likewise,
> Despite the similarity in size, the conditions differ, so a contrast transition is needed.

@ X
The ancient Romans used volcanic ash in their concrete. ______, many Roman harbors have survived two thousand years of seawater.
* Consequently,
- Nonetheless,
- In contrast,
- Similarly,
> The ash is presented as the reason the harbors survived, so a result transition fits.

@ X
Sea otters eat large numbers of sea urchins. Urchins, in turn, eat kelp. ______, where otters thrive, kelp forests tend to flourish.
* Thus,
- However,
- For instance,
- Meanwhile,
> The final sentence follows logically from the chain (otters eat urchins, which eat kelp). "Thus" signals the conclusion.

=== 5|Building: Mid-Frequency Words II|Lessons 2.16–2.30; precision between close synonyms.

@ C
The new manager tried to ______ tensions within the team by holding private meetings with each employee who had complained.
* alleviate
- perpetuate
- incite
- galvanize
> Private meetings with complainers are meant to reduce tension: "alleviate." "Perpetuate" and "incite" would increase it.

@ C
Each spring, the river rises and ______ the low-lying farmland, leaving behind a layer of nutrient-rich silt.
* inundates
- erodes
- depletes
- subsides
> Rising water that covers farmland floods it: "inundates." "Subsides" means to fall, and erosion would remove soil rather than leave silt behind.

@ C
The two accounts of the battle ______ sharply after the first day: one claims a decisive victory, while the other describes an orderly retreat.
* diverge
- coincide
- converge
- reiterate
> Accounts that describe different outcomes move apart: "diverge." "Converge" and "coincide" mean the opposite.

@ C
Although the vaccine's side effects were ______, appearing in fewer than one in a million patients, regulators required a warning label.
* sporadic
- detrimental
- rampant
- endemic
> Fewer than one in a million means rare and irregular: "sporadic." "Rampant" means the opposite.

@ C
The engineer's ______ design used a single moving part where earlier models needed twelve.
* ingenious
- cumbersome
- tedious
- craven
> Cleverly reducing twelve parts to one is inventive: "ingenious." "Cumbersome" is the opposite.

@ C
Critics argued that the statue's restoration had ______ the original, since bright modern paints replaced colors that had softened over centuries.
* distorted
- rectified
- commemorated
- emulated
> Replacing aged colors with bright modern ones changes the work for the worse: "distorted." "Rectified" would mean corrected.

@ C
The colony's population began to ______ after the harsh winter, falling from 300 settlers to fewer than 90.
* dwindle
- burgeon
- proliferate
- coalesce
> Falling from 300 to under 90 means shrinking gradually: "dwindle." "Burgeon" and "proliferate" mean to grow.

@ C
The professor's lectures were known for their ______: in fifty minutes she could explain a theory that textbooks spent a whole chapter on.
* succinctness
- verbosity
- incoherence
- dissonance
> Explaining in fifty minutes what textbooks take a chapter to cover shows brevity and clarity: "succinctness."

@ C
Because the drug's long-term effects were still unknown, the agency imposed a ______ on new prescriptions until further studies were finished.
* moratorium
- precedent
- hiatus
- stipulation
> A temporary ban on prescriptions is a "moratorium." "Hiatus" is a pause but is not imposed as a ban on an activity.

@ C
The biologist's first trip to the reef was ______: she discovered a new species of shrimp by accident while searching for something else.
* serendipitous
- arduous
- painstaking
- futile
> A valuable discovery made by accident is "serendipitous." "Futile" would mean the trip accomplished nothing.

@ M
After the strike, the union and the company tried to repair the rift that had opened between workers and management.
? As used in the text, what does the word "rift" most nearly mean?
* break in relations
- crack in rock
- financial loss
- shift in schedule
> Something that opens between people and needs repair is a break in their relationship. The geological sense does not fit.

@ M
The scientist was careful not to impart her own expectations to the volunteers, who were told nothing about the study's purpose.
? As used in the text, what does the word "impart" most nearly mean?
* communicate
- divide
- conceal
- reward
> She avoided passing on, or communicating, her expectations to the volunteers.

@ M
The candidate's speech was widely praised, but her opponents dismissed it as mere rhetoric.
? As used in the text, what does the word "rhetoric" most nearly mean?
* persuasive language lacking substance
- formal logic
- the study of grammar
- a sincere promise
> "Mere rhetoric" is dismissive: impressive-sounding words without real content.

@ M
The heavy rains will not abate until the storm system moves east on Thursday.
? As used in the text, what does the word "abate" most nearly mean?
* lessen
- begin
- spread
- intensify
> Rains that continue until the storm moves away will then let up. "Abate" means lessen.

@ T
The reviewer dismissed the novel's plot as ______, pointing out that its long-lost twins, secret inheritance, and last-minute wedding had appeared in countless earlier books.
* hackneyed
- provocative
- cryptic
- whimsical
> Plot devices seen in countless earlier books are overused: "hackneyed."

@ T
In her letter, the explorer adopts a ______ tone toward the dangers ahead, joking that the crocodiles "look more bored than hungry."
* blithe
- ominous
- despondent
- reverent
> Joking lightly about danger is carefree and unconcerned: "blithe." "Ominous" would stress the threat.

@ T
Although the critic admired the actor, her review was ______: she praised his voice but described his final scene as "unconvincing."
* equivocal
- effusive
- caustic
- unanimous
> Mixed praise and criticism leave her overall verdict uncertain: "equivocal." "Effusive" is all praise and "caustic" all criticism.

@ X
The plan was expensive and would take a decade to complete. ______, the city council approved it unanimously, citing the long-term savings.
* Even so,
- As a result,
- Similarly,
- In other words,
> Approval despite the cost and delay is a concession; "Even so" signals it.

@ X
Scientists once believed that the appendix had no function. ______, recent research suggests that it stores beneficial gut bacteria.
* However,
- Therefore,
- Likewise,
- For example,
> The recent research contradicts the earlier belief, so a contrast fits.

@ X
The ceramicist's early bowls were plain and unglazed. ______, her later works feature bright, layered glazes in dozens of colors.
* By contrast,
- Accordingly,
- In addition,
- That is,
> Plain early work set against bright later work is a direct contrast.

=== 6|Building: Mid-Frequency Words III|Lessons 2.31–2.45; the upper-mid list, where the SAT tests tone and fine distinctions.

@ C
The judge ______ the defendant after new DNA evidence proved that he had been in another city on the night of the crime.
* exonerated
- castigated
- incriminated
- reprimanded
> Proof that he was elsewhere clears him of blame: "exonerated." "Incriminated" is the opposite.

@ C
Her rise in the company was ______: within three years of joining as an intern, she was running its largest division.
* prodigious
- quiescent
- interminable
- cursory
> Rising from intern to division head in three years is remarkable and impressive: "prodigious."

@ C
The negotiators reached a ______ after twelve hours, with neither side willing to make the first concession.
* stalemate
- rejoinder
- forbearance
- unanimity
> Neither side willing to move means a deadlock: "stalemate." "Unanimity" would mean full agreement.

@ C
The architect's ______ attention to detail meant that even the door handles were custom-designed to match the building's curves.
* fastidious
- perfunctory
- desultory
- cursory
> Custom-designing even the door handles shows extreme care: "fastidious." The other three all mean careless or hasty.

@ C
Unlike the ______ silver of earlier coins, which bent easily, the new alloy was hard enough to resist wear for decades.
* malleable
- brittle
- impermeable
- porous
> Metal that bent easily is soft and shapeable: "malleable." "Brittle" metal breaks rather than bends.

@ C
The documentary avoids ______ its subject: although it celebrates the inventor's brilliance, it also shows his cruelty to his employees.
* lionizing
- criticizing
- documenting
- mentioning
> Including his cruelty alongside his brilliance keeps the film from glorifying him uncritically, so "lionizing" fits. It plainly does criticize, document, and mention him.

@ C
The once-crowded fishing village now seems almost ______, its streets empty and its boats left to rot on the beach.
* desolate
- verdant
- congenial
- luxuriant
> Empty streets and rotting boats describe a bleak, abandoned place: "desolate."

@ C
The witness's account was ______: it sounded convincing at first, but every detail fell apart under questioning.
* specious
- indubitable
- cogent
- veracious
> Plausible at first but false underneath is the exact meaning of "specious."

@ C
Biologists were surprised to find that the ______ seed, buried in a desert cave for two thousand years, still sprouted when planted.
* dormant
- incipient
- luminous
- restive
> A seed that is inactive for centuries but still able to grow is "dormant."

@ C
The politician's ______ answers frustrated reporters: asked about the budget, he spoke for ten minutes without stating a single figure.
* evasive
- trenchant
- forthright
- laconic
> Talking at length without answering the question avoids directness: "evasive." "Forthright" is the opposite, and "laconic" means brief.

@ M
The mayor tried to placate angry residents by promising to repave the street before winter.
? As used in the text, what does the word "placate" most nearly mean?
* calm
- deceive
- organize
- ignore
> A promise offered to angry residents is meant to soothe or calm them.

@ M
Years of drought had parched the valley, turning green pastures into cracked brown earth.
? As used in the text, what does the word "parched" most nearly mean?
* dried out
- flooded
- divided
- abandoned
> Drought turning pastures to cracked earth means that they were dried out.

@ M
The essayist's prose is so mellifluous that readers often fail to notice how radical her arguments are.
? As used in the text, what does the word "mellifluous" most nearly mean?
* smoothly pleasing
- difficult
- angry
- repetitive
> Prose pleasant enough to distract readers from radical content flows sweetly: "mellifluous."

@ M
Though he was gruff in public, colleagues knew the professor as a beneficent mentor who quietly paid students' conference fees.
? As used in the text, what does the word "beneficent" most nearly mean?
* generous and kind
- stern
- famous
- wealthy
> Quietly paying students' fees is an act of kindness. "Beneficent" means doing good, in contrast to "gruff."

@ T
The memo's ______ tone, with lines such as "This will be done by Friday. No exceptions.", left employees feeling that their input was unwelcome.
* peremptory
- conciliatory
- jocular
- diffident
> Commands that allow no discussion are "peremptory." "Conciliatory" and "diffident" are the opposite.

@ T
The biographer's ______ portrait of the scientist omits every failure and quarrel, presenting him as a flawless genius.
* hagiographic
- balanced
- caustic
- ambivalent
> A portrait that omits all faults to show a flawless hero is "hagiographic," meaning saint-like. "Balanced" contradicts "omits every failure."

@ T
The comedian's ______ wit, delivered with a perfectly straight face, made audiences laugh a beat after each line.
* droll
- strident
- morose
- vitriolic
> Dry, quietly amusing humor delivered deadpan is "droll."

@ X
The Library of Alexandria was one of the ancient world's great centers of learning. ______, no reliable record of its full collection survives.
* Unfortunately,
- Consequently,
- Similarly,
- Specifically,
> The lack of records is a regrettable contrast with the library's importance.

@ X
Most bird species build nests from twigs and grass. The edible-nest swiftlet, ______, constructs its nest entirely from its own hardened saliva.
* however,
- therefore,
- for example,
- moreover,
> The swiftlet is an exception to the general pattern, so a contrast is needed.

@ X
Lichens are not single organisms but partnerships between a fungus and an alga. ______, the fungus provides structure, while the alga produces food through photosynthesis.
* Specifically,
- Nonetheless,
- In contrast,
- Similarly,
> The sentence explains in detail how the partnership works, so "Specifically" fits.
